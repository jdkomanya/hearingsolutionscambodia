Frequently asked questions

Straightforward answers for families considering hearing assessment, hearing aids, tinnitus support, or children’s hearing care.

A hearing assessment usually begins with a conversation about your concerns, ear history, and listening needs. Suitable hearing checks are completed, then results are explained in simple language.

No. Hearing aids do not cure hearing loss or restore normal hearing. They can improve access to speech and environmental sounds when fitted and adjusted appropriately.

Adjustment varies. Many people need several weeks of regular use, follow-up fine-tuning, and communication practice to feel more comfortable.

Tinnitus support usually focuses on education, sound strategies, stress reduction, hearing management, and medical referral when warning signs are present. Avoid any service that promises a guaranteed cure.

Ask about children’s hearing care if there are concerns about speech, listening, school performance, frequent repetition, high TV volume, or repeated ear infections.
